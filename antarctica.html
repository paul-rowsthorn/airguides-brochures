About the Experience
Move with the continent, not against it

Antarctica shifts with weather, ice, water and light, revealing itself slowly to travellers willing to move with the conditions.

Timed for peak Antarctic summer, this 14-night voyage is built around long daylight, active wildlife, sculpted ice and the freedom to adapt each day around what the continent gives you.

Expect Zodiac excursions, shore landings, penguin colonies, seals, whales, glaciers and icebergs in one of the most remote environments on Earth. Some days may be calm and luminous. Others may be wild, cold and weather-driven.

With Martin beside you, Antarctica becomes a lesson in patience, observation and storytelling, shaped by one of the planet's great polar wildernesses.

Your Storyteller Guide
Martin Gregus

Martin Gregus

Wildlife Photographer and Cinematographer

Martin Gregus grew up in Bratislava, Slovakia, in a household full of artists. He learned photography from his father and was exploring local wildlife with a camera before most kids had decided what they wanted to do with their lives.

His work has taken him across all seven continents and some of the most remote ecosystems on the planet. He has earned two wins and two nominations at Wildlife Photographer of the Year, and his polar bear project, 33 Days Among the Bears, became an international story after National Geographic coverage.

His cinematography has contributed to productions for Disney, BBC and Netflix. In the field, Martin teaches patience, preparation and how to read wildlife, weather and light.

Why This Trip?
A proper expedition rhythm with only 12 guests

Hans Hansson gives the group a smaller, more responsive platform for exploring Antarctic waters. The route can adapt to the strongest wildlife, ice and light available each day.

Martin is part of the daily rhythm, from gear preparation and fieldcraft to composition, wildlife behaviour and image review. The trip is designed for wildlife lovers as much as photographers.

Antarctica remains weather-driven and unpredictable. Every landing and route decision is made around safety and conditions, preserving the expedition character of the journey.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
Where does the expedition start and finish?
The final embarkation and disembarkation port is still to be confirmed. Guests will receive exact joining details before booking travel.
What wildlife might we see in late January?
Peak summer can bring penguin colonies, Weddell, crabeater and leopard seals, seabirds and possible encounters with humpback, minke and orca whales. Wildlife is never guaranteed.
Is the route guaranteed?
No. Antarctica is weather-driven and ice-dependent. The captain and expedition team shape each day around safety, conditions, wildlife activity and landing permissions.
Can I join if I am not a photographer?
Yes. The trip is for wildlife lovers and adventurous travellers as well as photographers. Martin's guidance is useful at every level.
What fitness level is required?
Guests should be comfortable stepping in and out of Zodiacs, walking on uneven or snowy ground and spending time outside in cold, wet and windy conditions.
