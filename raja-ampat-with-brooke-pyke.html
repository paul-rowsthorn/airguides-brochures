About the Experience
The ocean's crown jewel, explored properly

Raja Ampat sits in Indonesia's remote West Papua, at the heart of the Coral Triangle. It is one of the most biodiverse marine ecosystems on earth, with reefs that feel impossibly full of colour, movement and life.

This expedition is designed for divers who want more than a quick visit. The route begins in Sorong, then moves through Dampier Strait, Wayag, Aljui Bay, Misool and back through the islands, balancing big coral landscapes, macro dives, lagoons, viewpoints and night dives.

The source itinerary includes around 30 dives across 10 days, plus snorkelling, lagoon cruises, beach visits, hikes, workshops and marine biology sessions. The pace gives you time to settle into the reef rather than chase a checklist.

Certified divers are the natural fit, and the source file notes Open Water level or equivalent as the minimum. Snorkellers can also join the adventure, subject to final trip terms and route planning.

Raja Ampat rewards patience: currents, reef walls, macro life, mantas, sharks and coral gardens all shift with the conditions.

Your Storyteller Guide
Brooke Pyke

Brooke is a multi award-winning Australian underwater photographer, dive instructor and marine expedition guide whose work is grounded in ocean conservation and thoughtful storytelling.

Originally from Melbourne, Brooke found her way back to photography through scuba diving, then spent years working as a dive professional across Thailand, Indonesia and Australia. Her first professional underwater photography role came on Ningaloo Reef, where she now lives and works.

Raja Ampat is personal for Brooke. She has led small-group diving expeditions here since 2019, shaped around slower dives, private charters, respectful wildlife encounters and hands-on image mentoring in one of the most visually rich ocean environments on the planet.

"Photography and art have the power to ignite a passion for conservation in the next generation." - Brooke Pyke
Why Book This Experience?
Because Raja Ampat is too good to skim

Many visitors come to Raja Ampat to tick off mantas, pygmy seahorses or famous dive sites. Brooke's approach is different: stay longer, go deeper and build enough context to understand what you are seeing.

With a private charter, a 12-guest group and Damai II's experienced captain and dive crew, the route can respond to weather, current and guest needs while still aiming for the quieter corners that make the region feel wild.

For photographers, the value is not just access to subjects. Brooke is there for camera decisions, field support, editing, one-on-one feedback and the practical craft of making images that tell a meaningful ocean story.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
Can you guarantee what we will see?
No. Raja Ampat is wild, and nature writes the script. The experienced captain, dive team and small-group liveaboard structure help create the best possible chances while keeping safety and animal welfare first.
Do I need to be a photographer?
No. Photography is optional. The trip suits divers who want world-class reefs, thoughtful guiding and a deeper connection to the ocean. Guests with cameras receive direct support from Brooke.
What certification is needed?
The source file says Open Water level or equivalent is the minimum. It also recommends Deep Diver specialty or Advanced Open Water, since some sites are best below 18 metres and current experience is useful.
Do I need insurance?
Yes. Comprehensive travel and dive insurance is essential, including scuba diving, medical emergencies, cancellations and other unexpected events.
What wildlife might we see?
Possibilities include oceanic mantas, reef mantas, turtles, octopus, sea snakes, wobbegong sharks, walking sharks, reef sharks, eagle rays, pygmy seahorses, nudibranchs, schooling jacks and soft coral gardens.
What camera should I bring?
A GoPro is a useful entry point. For more control, the source file recommends a compact or full-frame camera with manual controls, wide-angle or fisheye lens, underwater housing, spare batteries, SD cards and backup storage.
What is the water temperature?
The source FAQ lists typical water temperatures of 25 to 29 degrees Celsius. A full-length 3 mm wetsuit is recommended for warmth across multiple dives and protection from jellyfish or fire coral.
