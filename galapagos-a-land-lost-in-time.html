About the Experience
The Galapagos should not feel possible. That is the point.

A cluster of volcanic islands 1,000km from the nearest coast, cut off long enough for life to take its own path. Above and below the waterline, the Galapagos still feels like the beginning of the world.

Below the surface, that isolation created something found almost nowhere else: hammerhead sharks schooling in the hundreds, whale sharks drifting through open water, marine iguanas feeding on reefs, and sea lions that treat divers like playmates.

Your route sails north from San Cristobal to Wolf and Darwin for three full days at two of the most famous dive regions on Earth, then south through Fernandina, Isabela and Cousins Rock.

This is advanced diving in one of the most biodiverse marine environments on the planet, guided by two of Australia's most respected ocean storytellers.

Marine iguanas, blue-footed boobies, giant tortoises and volcanic shorelines make the land time as strong as the diving.

Your Expedition Team
Brooke Pyke and Nush Freedman

Brooke Pyke

Underwater Photographer

Brooke is an award-winning Australian underwater photographer whose work has taken her from Western Australia and Indonesia to Tonga and beyond.

In the water she brings a calm, intuitive approach that helps guests feel confident while creating images they are proud of. Marine conservation sits at the heart of how she teaches, shoots and leads.

Nush Freedman

Cinematographer

Nush is an underwater cinematographer, wildlife filmmaker, dive master and marine expedition guide based around Ningaloo Reef.

Her work in remote oceans has contributed to high-profile documentary productions for major international broadcasters and streaming platforms. On this expedition, she supports guests with video, storytelling and ocean awareness.

"Photography and art have the power to ignite a passion for conservation in the next generation." - Brooke Pyke
Why Book This Experience?
Built for divers who want more than a checklist

This is not a broad sightseeing cruise. It is a focused liveaboard expedition for confident divers who want proper time in the remote marine sites that made the Galapagos legendary.

The value is in the combination: a private liveaboard rhythm, the remote northern route, a small group, and guides who understand how to tell the story of the ocean while respecting the animals first.

Brooke and Nush are not there as distant names on the itinerary. They are part of the daily rhythm: field support, photo and video sessions, marine biology context, and a professional gallery that lets guests stay present in the water.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
How experienced do I need to be?
This is an advanced diving expedition. Guests should be comfortable with repeated dive days, current, blue-water entries, liveaboard routines and following dive guide instructions in remote conditions.
How many dives are planned?
The trip includes 19 planned dives, including Wolf and Darwin, Fernandina, Isabela, Cousins Rock and Pinzon. Final dive plans depend on weather, safety, park rules and conditions.
Do I need to be a photographer?
No. The trip is valuable for wildlife lovers and serious divers as well as photographers. The masterclasses are there to help guests improve, understand the species and bring home stronger stories.
What wildlife might we see?
Expect the possibility of hammerheads, whale sharks, Galapagos sharks, eagle rays, mobula rays, sea lions, marine iguanas, penguins, giant tortoises and blue-footed boobies. Wildlife is wild, so sightings cannot be guaranteed.
What is the group size?
The expedition is limited to 14 guests, keeping the experience personal and giving guests more direct time with Brooke, Nush and the dive team.
