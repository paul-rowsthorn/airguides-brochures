The Story
Deep into the wild heart of Patagonia

This is Patagonia at its most raw: granite peaks, sweeping valleys, open sky and pumas moving through one of the most dramatic backdrops on Earth.

The expedition begins in Puerto Natales with a welcome dinner hosted by Martin, then moves by private vehicle into Torres del Paine and onward to Laguna Amarga, your base for the puma days.

Over ten days, Martin helps the group read the landscape, anticipate wildlife behaviour and create images with intent rather than luck. The aim is not simply to see a puma. It is to understand how these animals live in this place.

Evenings bring the work to life through editing sessions, conservation discussion and story-led reviews with Martin and your Airguides expedition leader.

Puma encounters are wild and unscripted. Local trackers, Martin and the expedition team make daily decisions around weather, terrain, safety and animal behaviour.

Your Storyteller Guide
Martin Gregus

Martin Gregus is an internationally awarded wildlife photographer and cinematographer whose work has taken him across all seven continents.

Born in Bratislava, Slovakia, Martin grew up surrounded by artists and learned photography from his father. National Geographic magazines shaped his early imagination, and local wildlife became his first classroom.

Nineteen years later, he has earned two wins and two nominations at Wildlife Photographer of the Year. His 2022 project, 33 Days Among the Bears, became an international story after National Geographic coverage, and his cinematography has contributed to productions for Disney, BBC and Netflix.

"When you're standing on polar ice at sunrise, you don't need convincing that this planet is worth protecting." - Martin Gregus
Why Book This Experience?
Because puma photography is earned, not stumbled upon

A standard tour can take you through Patagonia. This expedition is built to slow you down inside the fieldcraft that creates real wildlife images.

Martin brings the perspective of someone who has spent years working in extreme wilderness. Local trackers bring intimate knowledge of the reserve, the terrain and the cats that move through it. Together, they help guests understand where to look, when to wait and when to move.

The landscapes matter too. Torres del Paine is not just a backdrop. Its wind, weather, light and distance shape every decision, and learning to work with those forces is part of the experience.

In The Field
Local trackers, private vehicles and days that follow the cats

The expedition exists here because the Torres del Paine and Laguna Amarga region holds some of the highest wild puma densities on the planet.

That does not make sightings guaranteed. It means the team has a serious foundation: local tracking knowledge, private reserve access, repeated days in the field and a guide who understands how to work calmly around wildlife.

Each day is practical. Wake early, move with purpose, watch wind and light, listen to local advice, and let the story come together one decision at a time.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
Will we definitely see pumas?
No. Martin, the local trackers and the location give the group a strong foundation, but pumas are wild animals. Every decision about where to go and when is made to give the group the best possible chance while respecting the animals and the landscape.
What level of fitness is required?
Moderate to good. You will spend five days trekking across uneven and occasionally steep terrain. If you plan to carry camera gear, factor that weight into your preparation.
Can I do this trip if I am not a photographer?
Yes. The expedition focuses on wildlife and landscape first. Martin's masterclasses are there for guests who want them, but travellers who simply want to watch pumas in one of the wildest places on Earth are welcome.
Do I need a visa?
Many nationalities can obtain a 90-day tourist visa on arrival in Chile. Check your own entry requirements before booking, as rules can vary by passport and change over time.
Is there a single supplement for solo travellers?
Yes. Solo travellers can request a private room for an additional fee. Airguides may also be able to pair solo travellers in shared rooms on a first-come, first-served basis.
Can Airguides help with flights?
Yes. The team can help with flight options and travel routing. Call, text or WhatsApp Airguides at +61 402 552 918 for planning questions.
