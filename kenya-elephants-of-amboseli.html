The Experience
The stage is Amboseli. The story is elephants.

Amboseli is one of Africa's great wildlife stages: vast open plains, elephant herds moving through dust, and Kilimanjaro rising behind it all.

Your journey begins in Nairobi with a welcome dinner where Chris shares what drew him to Africa and how he uses photography as a tool for conservation. From there, the group travels by private transfer to Osero Sopia Mt. Kili Camp, your base for seven safari days.

Each morning you head into Amboseli in private vehicles with Chris and expert Maasai guides. The focus is elephants, but the park's supporting cast is strong: zebras, cheetahs, giraffes, raptors, waterbirds and the changing mood of the plains.

Afternoons bring sundowner drinks, image reviews and masterclasses that go beyond camera settings. Chris will push your eye, your timing and your understanding of the animals you are photographing.

Amboseli gives photographers scale, behaviour and atmosphere in the same frame.

Your Guide
Chris Fallows
Storyteller Guide

Chris Fallows is a South African wildlife photographer and conservationist whose career has been built in the field, not from the sidelines.

For more than 30 years, Chris has documented wild places and the animals that define them. In 1996, he became the first person to photograph a great white shark breaching near Seal Island, South Africa, creating one of modern wildlife photography's iconic moments.

His work has appeared in Planet Earth, Shark Week and more than 500 publications worldwide. He has contributed to more than 60 international documentaries and is known for blending technical mastery with deep respect for his subjects.

On this journey, Chris brings the same patience, fieldcraft and conservation perspective to elephants, big skies and the daily rhythm of Amboseli.

Why Book This Experience
This is not a standard safari with a famous name attached

It is a small-field learning environment built around one of Africa's most photogenic elephant landscapes and a guide who understands how conservation, story and image-making connect.

With only eight guests, the group can move with purpose. Private vehicles mean less compromise in the field. Maasai guides bring local knowledge of Amboseli's animals, routes and communities. Chris brings the long view: how to anticipate behaviour, when to wait, when to move, and how to turn an encounter into a photograph that carries meaning.

The masterclasses are woven into the safari rather than bolted on at the end. You will work in the field, review what happened, refine your approach, and return with a clearer eye the next morning.

Amboseli is famous for elephants, but the real value is the depth: seven safari days to slow down, read the landscape and photograph with intention.

In The Field
Private vehicles, better decisions

The safari days use private 4x4 Land Cruiser vehicles, giving the group flexibility to work with light, animal behaviour and local conditions.

That matters in Amboseli. A strong elephant encounter can unfold slowly, and a rushed vehicle can break the rhythm. Private vehicles let Chris and the Maasai guides make field decisions around the group's photographic goals instead of a generic sightseeing timetable.

Expect early starts, long watches, quiet patience and practical in-field coaching. The goal is not only to see the animals, but to understand what is happening before pressing the shutter.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
Do I need to be an advanced photographer?
No. The trip is built for wildlife lovers and photographers who want to improve. Chris will adapt guidance to the group, from field technique to storytelling and editing.
What camera gear should I bring?
A camera with a telephoto lens is ideal for wildlife. A recommended gear list will be shared before departure, including practical guidance for dust, storage and field workflow.
Is the balloon safari included?
No. The balloon safari is optional and can be arranged separately, subject to availability, weather, safety and local operating conditions.
How physical is the trip?
Most safari time is spent in vehicles, with some camp walking, village visits and uneven ground. Guests should be comfortable with early starts and long days outdoors.
What wildlife will we see?
Amboseli is famous for elephants, with strong chances for zebra, giraffe, cheetah, hyena, raptors and rich birdlife. Wildlife is wild, so sightings cannot be guaranteed.
