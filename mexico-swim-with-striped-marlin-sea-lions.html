About the Experience
A feeding event found almost nowhere else

Every year, sardines gather off Mexico's Magdalena Bay and draw striped marlin, sea lions and seabirds into a high-speed hunt.

Each morning starts early. You will head offshore with Karim and a local captain, reading the surface for signs of activity. When the moment comes, you enter with snorkel gear and meet the action at eye level.

Some encounters are a flash of marlin in the blue. Others place you beside a bait ball as predators work from every direction. The animals decide what happens next, and the team follows strict guidelines to keep each interaction ethical and respectful.

Karim is beside you in the water and behind the lens, helping you make stronger images without losing sight of the experience itself.

Wildlife encounters are never guaranteed. That unpredictability is exactly what makes them wild.

"Swimming with marlins, sea lions and other animals is a transformative experience. We strive to share it in an intimate, respectful and engaging way."
Karim Iliya, Storyteller Guide
Your Storyteller Guide
Karim Iliya

Karim Iliya

Wildlife Photographer, Filmmaker and Conservationist

Karim's work offers a rare glimpse into the lives of some of Earth's most compelling species. From swimming with whales to documenting volcanic eruptions from above, his images have appeared in National Geographic, BBC Earth and other leading publications.

His approach combines technical mastery with patience, instinct and respect for the natural world. In Magdalena Bay, he offers one-on-one support in photography, storytelling and underwater ethics, whether you are an experienced shooter or using a camera underwater for the first time.

"Photography lets me see the Earth in a new way, where I take my time looking at the details." - Karim Iliya
Why This Trip?
Five days to follow the ocean, not a timetable

The sardine run is fast, raw and unpredictable. Repeated days offshore create time to read the conditions and be ready when the bay comes alive.

The local captain and crew know these waters. Karim understands both pelagic wildlife behaviour and what it takes to make purposeful underwater images. The five-guest maximum keeps the experience personal.

This is open-ocean snorkelling, not a staged wildlife encounter. The boat remains close, the crew monitors conditions, and the animals dictate every interaction.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
How strong a swimmer do I need to be?
You need to be a confident snorkeller in open water, but you do not need to be an elite swimmer. The boat stays close and the crew monitors conditions throughout.
What are the water conditions in November?
Water temperatures are typically 27 to 29 degrees Celsius, with average visibility of 10 to 15 metres. A 3 to 5mm wetsuit is recommended for longer sessions.
Is wildlife guaranteed?
No. The expedition centres on the sardine run, but sightings of any species cannot be guaranteed. Weather, ocean conditions and wildlife behaviour determine each day.
What gear should I bring?
Bring your own mask, snorkel, fins and wetsuit. No snorkelling gear is provided or available for hire. For photography, bring the camera setup you know best. A laptop with Adobe Lightroom can be useful for editing.
How do I get there?
Fly into La Paz International Airport or Los Cabos International Airport. The trip starts and ends in La Paz, with overland and boat transfers to Magdalena Island included from there.
What insurance is required?
Comprehensive in-water coverage is mandatory. Your policy must cover open-ocean snorkelling, medical expenses and emergency evacuation.
