About the Experience
A rare window into the warm waters of Vava'u

Each year, humpback whales travel from Antarctica into Tonga's warmer waters. For a short seasonal window, careful, regulated swim encounters can become possible.

This journey is built around that possibility, not a promise. The group heads out each whale day with experienced local operators who read the animals, the weather and the water before any entry is made.

Brooke's role is to help you move with intention. She guides water confidence, light, framing, distance and patience, so the goal becomes more than getting close. It becomes understanding what is happening in front of you.

Beyond the whales, the week includes blue caves, remote beaches, island viewpoints, shared meals and the warmth of the Tongan community that makes Vava'u feel so different from a standard marine tour.

Every in-water moment is conditional. Weather, visibility, local rules, guide judgement and whale behaviour decide what is possible.

Your Guide
Brooke Pyke

Brooke is an award-winning Australian underwater photographer whose work is rooted in conservation, animal-first encounters and calm confidence in the water.

Entirely self-taught, Brooke has worked across the reefs of Western Australia, Indonesia, Tonga and the wider Pacific. Her images have earned international recognition and appeared in ocean publications including Oceanographic and EzDive.

On this expedition, she brings the field sense of a wildlife photographer and the patience of someone who understands that guests arrive with different levels of confidence. Her guidance is steady, practical and always shaped by respect for the animals.

"Photography and art have the power to ignite a passion for conservation in the next generation." - Brooke Pyke
Why Book This Experience?
More patience, more context and more care

A whale swim is not better because it is rushed or crowded. The best moments come when the group, the crew and the guide are patient enough to let the animals lead.

Private boat days give the experience room to adapt. If the ocean asks for waiting, you wait. If the whales are moving, the team makes measured decisions. If the right encounter does not present itself, the animal-first call is still the right call.

That is where Brooke changes the trip. She helps guests see beyond the obvious frame: behaviour, light, movement, emotion and the responsibility that comes with entering another animal's world.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
Can you guarantee whale swims?
No. Humpbacks are wild animals and every day is shaped by weather, visibility, sea state, local regulations and whale behaviour. The private boat structure and experienced local operators help create opportunities, but animal welfare and safety come first.
Is swimming with humpback whales ethical?
Tonga has regulated whale swim guidelines, and Airguides works with operators who prioritise the whales' comfort. Entries only happen when the crew judges the situation to be appropriate. If a whale is not receptive, the group does not force the encounter.
Do I need to be a diver?
No. This is a snorkelling experience. Humpbacks come to the surface to breathe, and the focus is calm surface interaction rather than scuba diving.
How confident in the water should I be?
You should be comfortable snorkelling in open water and able to manage several hours around boats, ladders, swell and changeable conditions. Brooke and the local crew support the group, but this is still an ocean expedition.
Do I need to be a photographer?
No. Brooke's guidance is valuable for camera users, but non-photographers still benefit from her wildlife knowledge, calm water coaching and conservation perspective.
What is Vava'u like?
Vava'u is a low-key island group in northern Tonga, with clear water, green hills, local restaurants, cafes, markets and a warm community feel. It is beautiful, remote and less polished than a resort destination.
What should I bring?
Bring your own mask, snorkel, freediving or snorkelling fins, wetsuit, reef-safe ocean layers, seasickness medication if needed and a reusable water bottle. Photographers should bring their camera, underwater housing, spare batteries, SD cards, lens cloths and a laptop with Lightroom Classic if they want to join the editing workflow.
