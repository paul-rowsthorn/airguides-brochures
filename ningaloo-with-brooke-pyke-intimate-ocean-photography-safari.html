About the Experience
A postcard reef, treated like a classroom

This is not just a trip to collect beautiful images. Across a week on the UNESCO-listed reef, guests learn to use photography as a creative lens for understanding Ningaloo's marine ecosystem and their place within it.

Brooke has shaped the experience with the insight of a long-time Exmouth local. The pace is unhurried, curious and close, with time to understand the animals rather than chase a quick encounter.

The week begins with freediving foundations, then moves into four full private charter days. Exmouth brings whale shark and megafauna searching with a spotter pilot overhead. Coral Bay brings manta rays, turtles, reef systems and a quieter southern rhythm.

Every day is designed around better water confidence, better image-making and deeper context: marine biology, ethical encounters, editing, feedback and professional guidance.

Ningaloo is one of the world's great whale shark coastlines, but every encounter is still guided by weather, wildlife and respect for the animals.

Your Guide
Brooke Pyke

Brooke is an award-winning Australian underwater photographer whose work is rooted in Ningaloo, conservation and a calm, animal-first way of being in the water.

Originally from Melbourne, Brooke came to photography early through film, then found a new direction after a chance scuba diving encounter changed everything. She trained as a dive instructor, spent years working across Thailand, Indonesia and Australia, and built a career around showing people the beauty below the surface.

Her work has earned international recognition and her images have been featured in ocean publications around the world. On this trip, she is your teacher, field guide and creative lead, helping guests shoot, edit and understand the story behind the wildlife.

"Photography and art have the power to ignite a passion for conservation in the next generation." - Brooke Pyke
Why Book This Experience?
A reef safari built around access, not crowds

Public tours can mean sharing the water with large groups and moving on before an encounter has room to breathe. This trip was built because that version of Ningaloo was not enough.

With private charters, a small group and a spotter pilot overhead, each day can adapt to the reef. If whale sharks are moving, you move. If mantas are feeding, the day can slow down. If conditions change, the crew can make the best call for guests and wildlife.

The photography support also changes the experience. Brooke is in the water, at the table, in the review sessions and in the edit room. You are not just hoping for images, you are learning how to make them.

On The Water
Private charter days, guided by the reef

Four private charter days give the group space, flexibility and a better chance of meaningful encounters than a standard day tour.

Exmouth days focus on whale sharks and other megafauna with Latitude 22. Coral Bay days move south to manta rays, loggerhead turtles and reef systems, with Wirraji listed in the trip materials as Western Australia's first fully electric vessel.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
Can you guarantee wildlife?
No. This is a marine safari, and every day is guided by nature. The spotter pilot, private charter structure and experienced crew help maximise opportunities, but animal welfare and safety come first.
Do I need to be a photographer?
No. Photography is optional, but the trip is designed for people who want to understand the reef more deeply. Guests with cameras get direct support from Brooke, and non-photographers still benefit from the wildlife, ecology and ocean skills.
How strong a swimmer do I need to be?
You should be a confident ocean snorkeller with moderate swimming ability and fitness. Conditions may include wind, swell and open water, though guides and safety-trained crew stay close.
Do I need freediving experience?
No. The trip includes a full-day freediving foundations workshop. Diving down is optional, but the skills help with comfort, safety and camera movement in the water.
What wildlife might we see?
Possibilities include whale sharks, manta rays, humpback whales, turtles, reef sharks, stingrays, dugongs, dolphins, octopus, reef fish and macro life. Sightings cannot be guaranteed.
What camera should I bring?
A GoPro can work well for beginners. For guests with a larger setup, a compact or full-frame camera with underwater housing and a wide-angle or fisheye lens is ideal. Bring spare batteries, memory cards and backup storage.
