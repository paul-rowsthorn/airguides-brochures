The Story
One of the last places blue whales still feel truly wild

The waters off East Timor are one of the last places on earth where blue whales move through ocean that feels genuinely untouched.

They come here to feed, following deep currents along a coastline most travellers never reach. The group moves by boat, scanning for the long, low exhale that gives them away, then slipping into the water when Caine, the captain and the crew judge the moment to be right.

This is not a scripted swim. It is a wild ocean experience where timing, weather, visibility, animal behaviour and local knowledge shape each day. That is the point. The encounters matter because nothing is staged.

Beyond the water, the days settle into simple expedition rhythm: small group meals, late afternoons unwinding, conversation with people who have just shared something hard to explain to anyone who was not there.

Blue whale encounters are wild and conditional. Safety, weather, visibility, local rules and whale behaviour decide what is possible each day.

"I aim to give you much more than just seeing these animals and calling it a day. My guides and I know the difference between seeing these animals and truly experiencing wild."
Caine Delacy | Storyteller Guide
Your Storyteller Guide
Caine Delacy

Caine Delacy is an underwater photographer and marine biologist with a PhD, whose career has been built around documenting the state of the world's oceans.

His imagery has been featured in publications across Australia, the UK, South Africa and the US, including standout assignments for The New York Times.

As a guide, Caine brings more than camera advice. He understands animal behaviour, marine ecology and the responsibility that comes with entering the water around wildlife. His coaching works for guests picking up a camera for the first time and for photographers refining a craft they have been building for years.

"The ocean's been my passion since day one. Photography lets me dive deeper, sharing the untold stories of its inhabitants and the world they thrive in." - Caine Delacy
Why Book This Experience?
Because scale like this changes the way you see the ocean

Blue whales are the largest animals that have ever lived. Nothing prepares you for the scale of them up close.

This expedition is built for travellers who want more than a sighting. Caine and the local team help guests understand what they are seeing: the currents, feeding behaviour, approach decisions, distance, patience and quiet discipline that make wild encounters possible.

East Timor adds another layer. The coastline is raw, the culture is warm and the waters are some of the least-dived in the region. You come for the blue whales, but the place itself stays with you too.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
Who is this trip for?
Anyone who wants to spend time with blue whales and is comfortable in open water. You need to be able to swim confidently, snorkel comfortably and manage boat days where entries may involve swimming to and from the whales.
Do I need to be a photographer?
No. The trip is open to everyone who is confident in the water. Photography and videography workshops are optional, and the main focus is spending time with the whales respectfully.
Are we guaranteed to see whales?
No. This is nature, and it is wild. The product page notes that whales are there every year, but it also says there are no guarantees for how interactions will be. The team works to create the best opportunities while keeping the animals and guests safe.
What should I bring?
Bring a rash vest or thermal top, mask, snorkel, fins, rain jacket or waterproof shell and your camera if you plan to shoot. The product page notes water around 28 to 30 degrees Celsius, but ocean comfort still depends on each guest.
Who leads the trip?
Every Airguides expedition has an Airguides trip leader for logistics and a storyteller guide for the field experience. On this trip, Caine Delacy brings the photography, marine biology and wildlife behaviour expertise.
What marine life might we see?
The trip is built around blue whales. The live product page also notes other marine life and a high chance of seeing sperm whales too, but all wildlife sightings remain conditional.
