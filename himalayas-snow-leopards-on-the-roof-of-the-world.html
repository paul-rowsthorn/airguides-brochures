The Experience
One of the rarest cats on earth, on its own terms

Snow leopards are among the hardest animals on earth to find, let alone photograph. This journey is built around doing the search properly.

The expedition begins in New Delhi before flying into Leh, where the group takes time to acclimatise and explore the cultural edge of the Himalayas. From there, you move into snow leopard country with expert trackers, 4x4 support and Martin Gregus working alongside the group in the field.

Some days may deliver a direct encounter. Other days may bring tracks, calls from distant valleys, a kill site, or the ghost of movement across a ridgeline. That is the point. This is real wildlife work in high mountain terrain, with patience and preparation at the centre of the experience.

The expedition also moves east into Changthang, part of the Tibetan Plateau, where Pallas's cats, Tibetan wolves, Tibetan sand foxes and Tibetan gazelles shape a different kind of photographic challenge.

Snow leopards disappear into this terrain. Finding them takes local knowledge, time and a disciplined eye.

Nineteen years later, his work has taken him across all seven continents and into some of the most remote ecosystems on the planet. Why Book This Experience
This is not a distant sighting trip

The difference is structure: a small group, the right camp position, local trackers already scanning the ground, and Martin working with you before, during and after the field days.

Snow leopard photography asks for discipline. You need to read rock, snow, tracks, weather and silence. The expedition is designed around that reality. Each day begins with spotters moving early into the valleys, the camp waiting on live updates, and the group ready to respond when conditions change.

Martin's masterclasses are woven into the rhythm of the expedition. You will work on field technique, long-lens discipline, image review, editing decisions and the patience required to create work that carries more than proof of sighting.

Then the journey shifts to the Tibetan Plateau, where the target species change and the landscape opens out. Pallas's cats and plateau wildlife add depth to the trip, turning it from a single-species search into a broader Himalayan field story.

In The Field
The radio call changes everything

The rhythm is simple: trackers move first, the group stays ready, and the day follows the best information available.

Snow leopards do not operate on a tour schedule. Each morning, spotters position themselves across valleys and scan for movement, tracks and behaviour. When a credible update comes through, the group moves by 4x4 and on foot as conditions allow.

Some days will be about distance and patience. Some will be about being ready quickly. Quieter periods become part of the learning, with Martin working through long-lens choices, exposure in snow and rock, composition, behaviour and editing decisions.

This is fieldcraft as much as photography: knowing when to wait, when to move and how to stay calm when one of the world's rarest cats finally appears.

FAQ
What guests usually need to know
Are snow leopard sightings guaranteed?
No. These are wild animals in a vast mountain landscape. The itinerary, camp position and tracker network are designed to give the group the strongest possible chance of a real encounter, but sightings cannot be guaranteed.
How physical is the expedition?
Guests should be comfortable with high altitude, cold conditions, uneven ground and long field days. Some tracking involves walking, but movement is shaped by safety, conditions and the group's ability.
Do I need to be an advanced photographer?
No. The trip is built for wildlife lovers and photographers who want to improve. Martin's masterclasses cover field technique, image review and editing, with guidance adapted to the group.
What camera gear should I bring?
A camera body with a reliable telephoto lens is recommended. A detailed gear list will be shared before departure, including cold-weather battery, storage and layering advice.
What makes the Hanle section different?
Hanle and the Changthang region are more remote and simple than Leh or camp. The accommodation is local guesthouse style, chosen because it puts the group in the right landscape for Pallas's cats and plateau wildlife.
